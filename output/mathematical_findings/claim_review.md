# Mathematical scrutiny and revision record

This review covers the 11-page `posa_idea.pdf` supplied on 18 October 2026. Its prose and proposed procedures were treated as source material to evaluate, not as instructions to execute. The complete revised mathematical note is `main.tex`; its claims have proofs, stated observation assumptions, and a separate synthetic verification record.

## Main assessment

The core two-view interval and Fourier aliasing formulas survive scrutiny. The draft's interpretation of several formulas does not. In particular, geometric nonidentifiability does not prove inevitable failure of a population correction; the aspect ratio is not an odd-harmonic-only observable; and an embedded quadrature difference cannot certify the unknown residual without an additional assumption. The general width optimization also needs closed-class and attainment arguments rather than an assertion of compactness under strict positivity.

The revised paper replaces the angular-grid approach with a finite supporting-contact formulation. It gives the exact upper endpoint as a strip polygon and computes the lower endpoint with a second-order cone program. The same formulation accommodates width intervals. It also gives a simpler two-view proof, an exact linear witness family, and an explicit smooth counterexample to the proposed sampling certificate. All numerical examples in the new paper were generated from synthetic geometry in this workspace. The submitted human-scan and benchmark tables were not independently reproduced and are excluded from the short paper.

## Improvements developed during this review

1. **Short lower/upper proof.** Convex boundary coordinate variation is exactly twice each bounding dimension. A vector triangle inequality gives the lower bound and the coordinatewise norm inequality gives the upper bound. This replaces the longer reflected-contact argument.
2. **Exact witness family.** Minkowski interpolation of the endpoint rhombus and rectangle yields every intermediate perimeter by a linear formula. No nonlinear family fitting or quadrature is needed for this purpose.
3. **Finite sharp multi-view bounds.** Central symmetrization permits a centered strip polygon. Its perimeter is the exact upper endpoint. Supporting contacts give an exact finite SOCP for the lower endpoint and a polygonal extremizer. Exact consistent widths also give a closed upper formula.
4. **Interval observations.** The same contact construction includes bounded width uncertainty. Feasibility itself has a strip-polygon test, and upper bounds must account for redundant interval strips. This is the most useful candidate extension to develop further.
5. **An explicit view-design loss.** A largest-gap argument derives sharp unrestricted relative minimax risk, including a hard indistinguishable segment/polygon pair. For uniform k views the risk is tan squared(pi/(4k)). This is related closely to classical Crofton/zonotope bounds; it is not presented as a first-ever projection-quadrature theorem.
6. **A valid stopping rule.** An attainable interval gives deterministic absolute or relative stopping criteria, subject to validated numerical error allowances. The submitted half-difference alone does not.
7. **A smooth counterexample.** w(theta)=R+a(cos(8 theta)-1), with 0<a<R/64, is a valid strictly convex width function. All four relevant samples equal R, while its perimeter differs from the quadrature by pi a.
8. **Stable superellipse computation.** The optional helper integrates the dual-norm support h(theta)=[(a abs(cos theta))^q+(b abs(sin theta))^q]^(1/q), q=n/(n-1). This avoids differentiating the high-exponent boundary parametrization. The paper's interval witness does not depend on this helper.

## Novelty stance

The mathematical note is stronger and more concise than the submitted draft, but an unrestricted claim that sparse-projection perimeter estimation or its quadrature interpretation is new would be untenable. The prior work below overlaps directly with the problem setting and tools.

| Element | Position supported by this review |
|---|---|
| Cauchy formula, width/support theory, central symmetrization, zonoids | Classical foundation; cite Schneider. |
| Perimeter from incomplete projections | Explicit prior literature exists; see Baudrier et al. Their tomographic projection information is richer than a scalar width, so the observation models should not be conflated. |
| Sharp projection-quadrature relative bounds | Closely related established work by Kiderlen and Meschenmoser. The new proof states the loss and gives an indistinguishable-pair argument; broad priority is not claimed. |
| Supporting-contact formulation | Constructive reduction related to minimum-perimeter inscribed polygon problems; see Kodmon and Langi. Exact width-interval formulation and its integration into a reliable stopping procedure are the clearest candidate methodological contribution. |
| Superellipse support integration | Standard norm-duality and Cauchy tools yield a more stable evaluation method, rather than a new superellipse theory. |
| Human accuracy and cross-population transfer | No new claim is justified without original data/code and independent validation. |

This was a targeted literature review, not an exhaustive priority search. Before claiming a new theorem or submitting a novelty-led mathematical paper, compare the interval-uncertainty construction and the precise loss formulation against the full cited literature and obtain an independent mathematical review. The current draft consistently avoids unsupported priority language.

## Verification and limits

`verification_results.json` records all numbers generated here, including exact two-view checks, random-polygon containment, closed-upper consistency, interval widths, five sharp view-design witnesses, known superellipses, and the embedded-rule counterexample. `perimeter_bounds.py` is executable reference code; `verify_math.py` reproduces the synthetic checks with a fixed seed. No existing anthropometry application code was changed.

The conic formulation is exact as mathematics. Its implementation uses floating-point optimization. Small primal-dual residuals and gaps are checks, not rigorous interval-arithmetic enclosures. Any operational claim of guaranteed finite-precision stopping needs validated outward endpoint allowances.
