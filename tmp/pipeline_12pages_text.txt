Two-View Image-Based Anthropometry:
Proﬁle-Guided Reconstruction and Geometric Measurement
Author details to be completed
Abstract
This paper describes an anthropometry pipeline that converts frontal and proﬁle pho-
tographs into metric measurements using supplied stature. Pretrained segmentation and pose
estimation provide image evidence for an explicit geometric reconstruction. A proﬁle-guided
carve rejects displaced proﬁle runs and limits depth through frontal column thickness. A
curvature-dependent waist correction modiﬁes local occupancy, while an elliptical shoulder
model approximates an across-back path. The implementation speciﬁes coordinate calibration,
reconstruction branches, section conditioning, conic recovery, and local sensitivity calculations.
Historical pilot records from three adult male participants contain ten tape comparisons, with
a mean absolute error of 2. 69 cm. Veriﬁcation identiﬁes a sign discrepancy in conic-axis recov-
ery; the reported pilot values precede its correction. The proposed contribution concerns the
coupling of proﬁle ﬁltering with columnwise depth constraints and localized shape correction.
Its eﬀect on measurement accuracy remains to be established through corrected, independently
collected experiments.
Keywords: image-based anthropometry; visual hull; proﬁle-guided reconstruction; geometric
measurement.
1 Introduction and Related Work
Photographic anthropometry requires correspondence between image-derived geometry and a
speciﬁed physical measurement. ISO 8559-1 distinguishes circumferences, breadths, and surface
paths used in clothing measurement [ 1]. Meunier and Yin evaluated a two-dimensional pho-
tographic measurement system [ 2]; Hung et al. estimated circumferences from image-derived
widths and depths [ 3]. Automatic measurement from frontal and lateral silhouettes was subse-
quently developed by Aslam et al. [4]. These studies establish substantial prior work for two-view
measurement and elliptical section models.
The visual hull represents the volume consistent with observed silhouettes [ 5]. Human-shape
methods also include the statistical SMPL representation [ 6], volumetric inference in BodyNet [ 7],
and learned reconstruction from orthographic silhouettes [ 8]. The present pipeline uses pretrained
perception followed by geometric rules with explicit physical units. Its principal proposed adapta-
tion couples a ﬁltered proﬁle interval to frontal run topology and a columnwise thickness bound.
This construction addresses depth replication when a proﬁle silhouette contains arms or displaced
limbs. A spatially tapered waist correction provides a further local shape constraint. The shoul-
der model specializes an established ellipse-perimeter approximation to a deﬁned surface path.
These adaptations require ablation to establish an accuracy beneﬁt; the supporting segmentation,
ﬁtting, and meshing algorithms retain their established attribution.
The following formulation describes the implementation underlying the historical pilot records.
Chest is measured from the proﬁle-guided branch, whereas waist and hip use a corrected con-
ventional hull. A separately rounded proﬁle-guided volume supplies the display mesh. This
distinction is necessary because each operation imposes a diﬀerent section model. Geometric
processing uses ﬁxed rules after pretrained perception.

2 Image Preparation and Metric Alignment
2.1 Acquisition, masks, and landmarks
Inputs are frontal and approximately orthogonal proﬁle RGB images, together with stature H in
centimetres. The full crown and feet must be visible; close-ﬁtting clothing and consistent posture
reduce silhouette ambiguity. Let X denote lateral position, Y distance downwards from the crown,
and Z sagittal position, all in millimetres. A pinhole camera gives u = fxxc/z c + cu and v =
fyyc/z c + cv. Replacing depth by a representative z0 yields the weak-perspective approximation
used below. A depth displacement ∆ z changes magniﬁcation by approximately − ∆ z/z 0. The
implementation estimates neither camera intrinsics nor residual yaw, so stature calibration alone
does not eliminate perspective error.
The implementation uses MediaPipe [ 9] and its Pose Landmarker segmentation output [ 10].
The heavy pretrained model operates in single-image, single-person mode with detection and
presence thresholds of 0. 5. Contiguous RGB arrays are padded on the right with replicated
columns to a width divisible by four; the segmentation map is cropped back afterwards. This
alignment step preserves image scale. A mismatched map is resized bilinearly. Foreground is
deﬁned by the strict probability threshold p > 0. 5, followed by selection of the largest eight-
connected component. Enclosed background holes smaller than 2% of foreground area are ﬁlled;
border-connected background is retained. Missing pose or segmentation output, or foreground
occupying less than 2% of the frame, causes rejection. Default mask dilation is zero.
An optional GrabCut baseline uses the foreground/background colour-mixture and boundary
formulation of Rother et al. [11]. Landmark-based initialization constructs a torso polygon, limb
capsules, and a head region. With estimated pixel stature Hp = ( yankle − yshoulder)/ 0. 779, seed
erosion is 0. 012Hp and the probable-foreground and background dilations are 0. 045Hp and 0. 13Hp.
A border ring spans 2% of the smaller image dimension. Five iterations with random seed zero
precede a 7 × 7 elliptical closing and the same component cleanup. This baseline is separate from
the default learned mask.
Landmarks are reﬁned by a second inference on the silhouette bounding box expanded by 15%.
Crops whose longer dimension is below 1024 pixels are enlarged bicubically; larger crops retain
their resolution. For resize factor γ, resized crop dimensions (Wr, H r), and crop origin (x0, y 0), a
normalized landmark (ξ, η) maps to (x0 + Wrξ/γ, y 0 + Hrη/γ ). Shoulder, hip, and ankle pairs
(indices 11–12, 23–24, 27–28) determine alignment. Elbows and wrists provide subsequent overlap
checks. The ﬁt is rejected when more than three requested landmarks have visibility below 0. 3.

3 Reconstruction and Local Shape Constraints
3.1 Conventional occupancy and branch selection
For binary masks Mf , M s, the conventional orthographic occupancy is
O0(X, Y, Z ) = Mf (uf , v f )Ms(us, v s). (4)
At a given height, every frontal run pairs with every proﬁle run. With n frontal and m proﬁle
intervals, this produces up to nm rectangular regions, including limb-depth replication. Recon-
struction ﬁrst uses a 10 mm grid over silhouette extrema with 30 mm lateral and sagittal margins
and 0 ≤ Y < H m. The occupied coarse bounding box is expanded by one coarse cell and resam-
pled uniformly at h = 2 mm. Features absent from the coarse sample can consequently be missed.
The ﬁne stage stores a dense array over this box.
The largest 26-connected volume component is retained. Within successive XZ slices, eight-
connected components are tracked greedily by nearest centroid, with a maximum linking distance
of 60 mm. Tracks spanning at least ﬁve slices have their centroid coordinates and four half-extents
ﬁltered by a cubic Savitzky–Golay ﬁt [ 13], using at most 15 slices. Negative half-extents are
clipped to zero. Each original component is intersected with its smoothed bounding rectangle,
and the largest volume component is selected again. This operation trims occupancy; it cannot
recover missing material.
The resulting conventional hull receives the waist correction below and supplies waist and hip
sections. Chest uses an independently constructed, unrounded proﬁle-guided volume, followed
by its own section conditioning. A rounded copy of the proﬁle-guided volume is used for display.
Failure to construct a usable proﬁle core sends chest and display processing to the corrected
conventional hull. Shoulder and hip-to-ankle outputs use masks and landmarks directly.

3.3 Measurement levels and curvature-dependent waist correction
The frontal torso trace starts from the widest horizontal run and subsequently selects the run
nearest the preceding midpoint. Separate left/right arm-separation tests use ﬁve-row margins.
Missing trace values are interpolated and smoothed by a cubic 21-row ﬁt. With mean shoulder
and hip levels Ys, Y h, the waist proxy is Yw = Ys + 0. 688(Yh − Ys), snapped to the nearest available
trace row. The factor approximates (0. 818 − 0. 620)/ (0. 818 − 0. 530) from engineering body-height
fractions. Chest is evaluated at Yc = Ys + 0. 35(Yw − Ys); hip uses the landmark level. These
prescribed levels do not search for maximum chest or hip girth.
Each torso half-width is clipped using its 15th percentile in a ± 40 mm neighbourhood; non-
positive widths are rejected. Girth extraction expands these bounds by a factor of 1. 15, whereas
shoulder extraction uses the unexpanded trace. Conventional-hull sections also receive a centred
depth cap Dc = min( D, 0. 75W ) when a proﬁle elbow or wrist lies within 90 mm of the measure-
ment level, or when those landmarks are unavailable. Removal exceeding 1% of original depth
generates an advisory. Proﬁle-guided chest receives only lateral torso clipping.

4 Section Measurement and Surface Export
4.1 Conditioning and conic recovery
The nearest XZ slice is selected for each measurement level, giving a level quantization error
no greater than h/ 2 within the sampled range. After torso clipping and any conditional depth
cap, an OpenCV elliptical closing with nominal radius 4 mm ﬁlls small gaps. The largest eight-
connected section component is retained and opened with radius r = 0. 9 min(W/ 2, D/ 2), rounded
to grid units with a minimum of two pixels. In set notation, closing is (S ⊕ B) ⊖ B and opening
is (S ⊖ B) ⊕ B. The latter imposes substantial section rounding, which inﬂuences the reported
circumference.
All contour points are retained, and the contour of largest area is converted to metric (X, Z )
coordinates. A Graham scan [ 14] deduplicates points, sorts by polar angle, retains the farther
collinear point, and removes non-left turns. Complexity is O(n log n). At least ﬁve hull vertices
are required. Convexiﬁcation discards section concavities before ﬁtting.

4.2 Shoulder path and linear proxy
At the engineering acromion level Ya = 0 . 182Hm, let W, D be unexpanded traced width and
proﬁle depth. When W, D > 0 and D/W ≤ 0. 6, shoulder length is S = 1
2 P (W/ 2, D/ 2). This half-
perimeter assumes that shoulder endpoints coincide with the transverse extrema of a symmetric
section and that the posterior path is elliptical. Its contribution is a speciﬁed path model with
explicit applicability checks; a tape trajectory can diﬀer from these assumptions.
When this path cannot be computed, frontal silhouette tips are sought between Ys − 20 mm
and Ys + 0. 4(Ye − Ys), where Ye is the lower available elbow level. Missing elbows give an upper
limit Ys + 0. 18(Yh − Ys). The trace retains the arm-fused preﬁx, applies a quadratic Savitzky–
Golay window of at most nine rows, and selects each half-width maximum. A post-peak taper
of at least 5 mm on each side marks reliable tips. Their two-dimensional Euclidean separation
gives projected breadth; a tip-to-landmark breadth ratio outside [1, 1. 5] is advisory. Otherwise
the fallback is the shoulder-landmark chord multiplied by sf . The legacy fallback label can cover
either breadth method, so the method must accompany the value. The output historically named
inseam is Yankle − Yhip, a vertical hip-to-ankle proxy without a corresponding tape reference in
this pilot.
4.3 Display geometry and retained outputs
Display rounding is applied to a separate volume. Each XZ component is opened with a Euclidean
distance-transform element of radius 0. 9 times its smaller half-extent, with a two-pixel minimum;
an empty opening retains the component. Limb seeds satisfy frontal vertical thickness divided by
proﬁle-core depth < 0. 5, grow through ratios < 0. 85, and require frontal area at least 2000 mm2.
Limb Y Z openings are united with the rounded XZ result. Distance-transform erosion uses
dbackground > r and dilation uses distance to the eroded set ≤ r, with padding exceeding r. These
elements diﬀer from the rasterized OpenCV elements used for section measurement.
Junction repair attempts chessboard-distance closings of 20, 40, 60, and 100 mm, intersecting
the result with the unrounded proﬁle volume. Processing stops at one component or residual
components totalling at most 0. 5%. The largest component is exported, with an advisory if more
than 1% is removed. Zero padding of ⌈3σg⌉+ 1 voxels precedes Gaussian smoothing with σg = 1
voxel and marching cubes at isovalue 0. 5 with spacing h [17]. Origin and padding oﬀsets are
undone, and (x, y, z ) maps to (x, z, y max − y) for a ﬂoor-based, vertical- Z display frame. The
largest mesh component is retained; a watertight mesh with negative signed volume is inverted.
Twelve Taubin passes [ 18] use λ = 0 . 5 and ν = − 0. 53 under the applicable library convention.
Optional simpliﬁcation targets 150 000 faces.
Saved outputs include normalized masks, conventional and display occupancy, and measure-
ment records containing levels, ﬁtted axes, rotations, and shoulder method. Advisory messages
are largely logged and are not all serialized into the historical JSON. Reproducibility therefore
requires retaining the diagnostic log with image/model identiﬁers and numerical settings. Dense
Boolean occupancy consumes approximately NX NY NZ bytes; each full double-precision coor-
dinate array adds eight times this storage. Chunked evaluation limits temporary arrays while
leaving dense output storage unchanged.

6 Historical Pilot Results and Limitations
The pilot comprises three adult male project authors, of stature 172, 177, and 173 cm. Their
image dimensions were 1086 × 1448, 960 × 1280, and 900 × 1600 pixels, respectively. Acquisition
was unblinded, without independently repeated capture, repeated operators, or camera calibra-
tion. Table 1 reproduces the available comparisons from the historical implementation. Ranges

Across these ten comparisons, mean absolute error is 2.69 cm, root-mean-square error 3.13 cm,
signed bias − 1. 48 cm, and mean absolute percentage error 3. 39%. The maximum absolute error
is 5.83 cm. The eight girth comparisons alone have mean absolute error 2.81 cm. Repeated body
sites within participants are correlated; pooled summaries therefore describe these records and
cannot establish population performance. Only two references fall within estimate ± δ, consistent
with incomplete error coverage. Three segmentation repetitions for each of the six images were
pixel-identical in the tested environment. This demonstrates same-input repeatability under those
conditions; acquisition repeatability remains unmeasured.
The shoulder-path estimates were 44.71 and 48.81 cm against references of 48.00 and 47.62 cm,
while landmark chords were 36.52 and 30.39 cm. These examples illustrate the consequence of
using a surface-path model and do not establish its general validity. Agreement studies require
consistent measurement deﬁnitions and suﬃciently independent observations [ 20]. Corrected
end-to-end results are needed before attributing discrepancies to the geometric priors, especially
because conic-axis recovery aﬀects both estimates and conic-derived sensitivity.
The main unresolved question is whether the proﬁle/thickness coupling improves measurement
at occluded levels across postures and body shapes. An ablation should compare conventional
occupancy and ﬁltered proﬁle occupancy with and without the column bound, while holding per-
ception, level selection, and section ﬁtting ﬁxed. A corresponding waist comparison should sepa-
rate curvature displacement from section morphology, since both alter perimeter. The half-ellipse
shoulder path requires tape endpoints matched to its assumed transverse extrema. Independent
participants and repeated acquisition are necessary to separate systematic geometric error from
mask and capture variation. The present pilot cannot support a claim of general accuracy or
incremental improvement for any proposed component.
7 Conclusion
The pipeline provides a reproducible geometric speciﬁcation for deriving anthropometric esti-
mates from two calibrated silhouettes. Its proposed contribution lies in proﬁle-core reconstruction
constrained by frontal column thickness and a localized curvature-dependent waist adjustment.
Branch-speciﬁc section extraction and an explicit shoulder-path model make the measurement
assumptions inspectable. Historical pilot data illustrate implemented behaviour, while conic
veriﬁcation identiﬁes a numerical correction required before further evaluation. Independent ex-
8

periments with controlled ablations are needed to determine the value of the proposed geometric
adaptations.
